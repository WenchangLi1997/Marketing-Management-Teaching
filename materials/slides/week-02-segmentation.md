# Week 2: Segmentation Concepts and Case

## Marketing Principle #1: All customers differ

Customers vary in what they need, what they value, how they choose, and how they respond to marketing activity. The managerial task is to identify the differences that should change the firm's decisions.

## Learning objectives

- Explain why customer heterogeneity is a fundamental marketing problem.
- Distinguish customer needs from variables that merely describe customers.
- Use segmentation, targeting, and positioning to build a coherent strategy.
- Evaluate whether a segment can support action and economic value.

## Sources of customer heterogeneity

- Individual differences
- Life experience
- Functional needs
- Identity and self-image
- Marketing activity

## Latent heterogeneity

Different preferences may exist even when current market choices do not reveal them. Regulation, cost, technology, or a lack of innovation can prevent customers from expressing what they want.

## Evolution of segmentation

1. Mass marketing: one offering for a broad market.
2. Niche marketing: specialized offerings for narrower groups.
3. One-to-one marketing: personalized decisions at the individual level.

Smaller segments improve customer fit, but they also increase complexity and may reduce scale efficiency.

## STP

### Segmenting

Describe groups whose members seek similar benefits.

### Targeting

Select groups where the firm can create and capture value.

### Positioning

Establish a relevant advantage in the target customer's mind.

## Needs and descriptors

Needs and benefits should define segments. Observable descriptors such as age, location, company size, or behavior help identify and reach those segments. A demographic group is not automatically a meaningful market segment.

## Segmentation process

1. Use qualitative research to identify purchase attributes.
2. Survey a relevant sample of potential customers.
3. Group related attributes when necessary.
4. Group customers according to their needs.
5. Describe, name, and assess the resulting segments.

## Targeting criteria

Target selection combines market attractiveness with competitive strength. Market attractiveness includes size, growth, willingness to pay, and margins. Competitive strength captures the firm's ability to meet the segment's needs better than competitors.

## Positioning statement

A positioning statement answers three questions:

- Who is the target customer?
- What need or benefit does the offering fulfill?
- Why is the offering better than the alternatives?

## DentMax case

DentMax identified four segments:

| Segment | Share | Main priorities |
|---|---:|---|
| Loyalists | 12% | Image quality and diagnostic flexibility |
| Switchables | 22% | Software integration and technical assistance |
| Generalists | 49% | Price and technical assistance |
| Apathetics | 17% | Low involvement |

### Discussion

1. Which segment should DentMax prioritize?
2. Where does DentMax have a credible relative advantage?
3. What additional evidence would you collect?
4. Write a positioning statement for the selected segment.

## Takeaways

- Customer differences matter when they imply different value propositions or actions.
- Needs should define segments; descriptors help the firm identify and reach them.
- Targeting combines market opportunity with relative competitive strength.
- Positioning expresses a specific advantage for a specific target.
